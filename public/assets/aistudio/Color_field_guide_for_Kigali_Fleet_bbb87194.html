Where each color goes

A fleet app needs only a handful of color roles. Give each role one job, and never let two roles share a color. Most of the screen should be neutral: about 80 to 90 percent, then one action color, then status colors only where the data needs them.

The three-color rule for screens

If you squint at a screen, you should see mostly white, then black text, then one accent. Status colors are small dots and words. If two big areas of color compete, the screen is shouting.

The amber problem, measured

Bright amber looks warm on a dark screen, but it’s weak as text on white, and it’s doing five jobs in your app: buttons, links, tabs, “on trip” status and warnings. Test any pair below. Normal text needs 4.5 or higher; large text and borders need 3.

Where authentic colors come from

Authentic doesn’t mean copying a flag or a tourist poster. It means picking colors people in Kigali already see every day, then using them with restraint. These are sources, not a final palette. Hex values are close approximations, so check your own brand files.

Rwandan flagSky blue, sun yellow, green. Strong for brand moments; clashes with status meaning.
Lake KivuDeep teal-blue water and pale shore. Calm, readable, works for buttons.
Imigongo artBlack and white geometry with a rust or ochre. Excellent for a bold, flat identity.
Red earthLaterite soil and brick. Good for warm backgrounds and illustration, not for text.
Tea and NyungweHighland greens. Beautiful, but too close to your “Ready” green.
JacarandaViolet-blue blossoms along Kigali streets. Distinct from every status color.

Rules for a theme picker

  • Show 4 to 6 choices, not 10 or more.
  • Pick hues far apart, so a choice feels like a choice.
  • Never offer red or green as an accent. Those two belong to status.
  • Show the full name; wrap rather than cut.
  • Accent affects buttons, links and tabs only.
  • Light and Dark stay a separate switch.

Dark mode, briefly

  • Don’t invert colors. Re-pick each one: the action color usually needs to be lighter, and status colors lighter and a little less saturated.
  • Use dark blue-grey, not pure black, for the page, and a slightly lighter grey for panels.
  • Text is off-white, not pure white, so it doesn’t glow.
  • Check contrast again. Amber tends to pass on dark, which is why it looks good there and fails on white.
  • Offer light as the default for office use in daylight, and dark as an evening option.

Practice

  1. Take one screen of your app and turn everything grey except status dots and one button. Notice how much easier it is to read.
  2. Test your amber button text and amber links in the checker above.
  3. Pick two of the palettes, apply them to the same screen, and ask three staff members which they find clearer.

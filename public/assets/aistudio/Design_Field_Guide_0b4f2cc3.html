Try it: change the controls and see what makes text comfortable. Good defaults are 16–18px, line height 1.5–1.7, and 45–75 characters per line.

Kigali’s hills make driving an adventure. A good fleet manager checks insurance dates early, keeps spare keys in a safe place, and calls drivers before every long trip. When text lines run too long, readers lose their place; when they run too short, the eye jumps constantly. Test with real sentences, not placeholder text.

Hierarchy comes from size and weight together. If everything is bold, nothing stands out.

Color and spacing

Start with one neutral, one text color, one accent. Add status colors only when the data needs them.

Background #f3f5f8
Text #16202e
Accent #2447d6
Success #1f8a5b
Error #c9362f

Text needs a contrast ratio of at least 4.5 to 1 against its background. Light grey on white fails that test more often than people expect.

Spacing steps in multiples of 4 or 8 keep layouts tidy:

4px: icon to label
8px: related items
16px: inside cards
24px: between groups
48px: between sections
96px: page-level breathing room

Avoiding the generic, machine-made look

Generated designs share habits. None is wrong alone; the problem is using them by default regardless of the subject.

Common tells

  • Every section is the same rounded card with the same soft shadow.
  • Small ALL-CAPS label above every heading.
  • One word of every headline colored or italicized.
  • Gradient glows and purple-to-blue washes.
  • Numbered 01 / 02 / 03 for content that has no order.
  • Fade-and-slide animation on every element.
  • Stock phrases like “seamless” and “empower your workflow”.

What human work does instead

  • Starts from the real subject: plates, routes, mobile money, local places.
  • Uses real photos, real names, real prices.
  • Varies shape and size by importance, not uniformly.
  • Picks one memorable element and keeps the rest quiet.
  • Writes plain labels: “Add car”, not “Get started today”.
  • Moves things only in response to a click.
  • Tests on a cheap phone with sunlight on the screen.

Practice

  1. Pick one of the five page styles and rebuild one Kigali Fleet screen in it. Keep the data the same.
  2. Limit yourself to two fonts and three sizes. See how far hierarchy alone can go.
  3. Screenshot a site you admire, then list its fonts, colors, and spacing. Copy the structure, not the look.
  4. Print a screen in black and white. If the hierarchy still reads, your design is sound.
